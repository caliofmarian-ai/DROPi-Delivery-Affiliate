# DROPi Delivery — Business Model

## Positioning

DROPi Delivery is not a courier operator and does not need to own inventory. It is a specialist publication and comparison funnel serving two adjacent audiences:

1. Delivery workers: drivers, van couriers, food couriers and bike/e-bike couriers.
2. Small online sellers: merchants who need packing, labels, shipping software and dispatch workflow improvements.

This gives the project two monetisation layers without stock risk: physical-product affiliate commissions and higher-value SaaS referrals.

## Revenue ladder

### Layer 1 — high-intent physical gear

Content targets problems with clear product intent: phone mounts, chargers, power banks, waterproof gear, thermal bags, dash cams, trolleys, parcel scales and thermal printers.

The goal is not to rank for generic “best products”. The goal is to own narrower searches where the reader already has a delivery workflow problem.

### Layer 2 — ecommerce software referrals

Shipping software can generate more revenue per successful referral than low-cost accessories. DROPi should build authoritative articles around dispatch workflows, carrier integrations, returns and automation, then refer qualified readers to approved programmes such as Sendcloud and ShipStation.

### Layer 3 — email audience (later)

Only after the site has recurring traffic: a weekly “Route & Dispatch Brief” can distribute new buying guides, seasonal gear checks and shipping-software updates. Do not add an email form until a compliant email provider and consent flow are selected.

### Layer 4 — direct commercial partnerships (later)

Once traffic is measurable, approach relevant brands for fixed-fee sponsorship or exclusive reader offers. Sponsored placement must stay visibly separate from independent editorial ranking.

## North-star metrics

- Organic search impressions and qualified visits.
- Click-through rate from guide to commercial outbound link.
- Earnings per 1,000 content sessions.
- SaaS referral conversion rate.
- Percentage of revenue coming from more than one partner.
- Returning visitor rate once enough content exists.

## What not to do

- Buy inventory before demand is proven.
- Publish hundreds of thin AI-generated product pages.
- Claim products were physically tested when they were not.
- Depend on one affiliate network.
- Use paid ads directly into an affiliate link where partner terms prohibit it.
- Publish a price unless there is a reliable process to keep it current.
