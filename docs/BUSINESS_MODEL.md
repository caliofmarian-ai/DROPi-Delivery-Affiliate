# DROPi Delivery — Business Model

## Positioning

DROPi Delivery is a commerce-first buying and savings platform for delivery workers and small online sellers in Ireland and, later, the wider EU.

The site is **not** built around the assumption that large numbers of people will read long blog posts. Existing editorial guides remain useful as searchable support content, product education and trust infrastructure, but they are not the primary business model.

Primary audiences:

1. Delivery workers: drivers, van couriers, food couriers and bike/e-bike couriers.
2. Small online sellers: merchants who need packing, labels, shipping software and dispatch workflow improvements.

## Commercial engine

DROPi uses a hybrid monetisation hierarchy:

### Layer 1 — Shopify direct sale / dropshipping

If DROPi can sell a product itself at a verified positive unit profit, the primary commercial CTA should send the customer to the DROPi Shopify product page and checkout.

The supplier may fulfil the order directly. DROPi remains responsible for the customer-facing sale and must not publish a product until the supplier, fulfilment route, delivery terms and economics are known.

### Layer 2 — affiliate fallback

If DROPi cannot sell a product itself profitably or safely, an approved affiliate partner may be used instead. Affiliate links must never be fabricated and may only be activated after programme approval and tracking validation.

### Layer 3 — SaaS and qualified leads

Shipping software and other relevant commercial services may provide higher-value referral or lead revenue than small physical accessories. Examples include approved programmes for dispatch, fulfilment, ecommerce and courier-related services.

### Layer 4 — bundles and repeat-purchase systems

Where several products solve one workflow, DROPi should prefer kits and bundles such as driver starter kits, bike courier rain kits, food-delivery kits and small-dispatch workstation kits. This increases order value while keeping each component independently useful.

### Layer 5 — direct commercial partnerships

Once real usage and sales data exist, DROPi may approach relevant brands or suppliers for negotiated pricing, exclusive offers, wholesale terms or sponsored placement. Sponsored placement must remain clearly identified.

## Profit acceptance rule

A product is eligible for direct Shopify sale when the estimated **unit profit is at least EUR 2.00 after all known direct per-order costs**.

Unit profit formula:

`customer price - supplier product cost - supplier/customer shipping - payment/Shopify transaction cost - customs/import cost attributable to the order - other unavoidable direct order cost`

Policy:

- `< EUR 2.00` estimated unit profit: reject for direct sale; consider affiliate fallback.
- `>= EUR 2.00` estimated unit profit: eligible candidate.
- Unknown shipping, tax, customs or fulfilment cost: status remains `PENDING`, not approved.
- Higher-margin products are useful but are not required if a low-margin product is genuinely profitable and operationally sound.
- Bundles may combine multiple low-margin products where total order profit is materially better.

The EUR 2 threshold is a minimum economic gate, not a guarantee that every qualifying product should be published. Product safety, returns, reliability and delivery quality still apply.

## Sourcing policy

Priority order:

1. Supplier inventory already inside the EU with direct Shopify/dropshipping fulfilment.
2. Reliable European supplier/marketplace with clear shipping to Ireland and tracking.
3. Non-EU direct fulfilment only when the full landed cost remains profitable and compliance is clear.

From 1 July 2026 the EU applies a temporary EUR 3 customs duty per tariff-category item to relevant low-value consignments up to EUR 150 entering from outside the EU. This cost must be included in landed-cost calculations where applicable.

Official EU source: https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/guidance-and-legal-text-temporary-flat-fee-low-value-imports-which-will-apply-until-1-july-2028-2026-06-08_en

## Market-price discipline

Klarna Shopping Search is used as a market-price radar, not as the dropshipping supplier.

For each product candidate:

1. observe live Irish retail price ranges via Klarna and other current retailers;
2. obtain real supplier cost and delivery-to-Ireland cost;
3. calculate unit profit;
4. reject products that cannot meet the EUR 2 minimum after known direct costs;
5. only then create/activate the Shopify listing;
6. re-check market price and cost periodically.

DROPi should not deliberately overprice a commodity product when a materially cheaper equivalent is readily available to Irish buyers.

## Distribution model

The business must not depend on publishing thousands of generic articles or on Google search alone.

Potential acquisition channels include:

- product-led pages and comparison tools;
- social short-form content;
- courier and small-seller communities where promotion is permitted;
- direct partnerships;
- repeat users, alerts and saved shopping workflows;
- search and AI discovery as secondary channels;
- referrals from existing customers.

## North-star metrics

- Shopify gross sales and orders.
- Verified unit profit by SKU.
- Total contribution profit after direct order costs.
- Conversion rate from DROPi product/comparison page to Shopify checkout.
- Average order value and units per order.
- Return/refund rate by supplier and SKU.
- Supplier delivery reliability to Ireland.
- Affiliate revenue only where direct sale is unavailable or inferior.
- Revenue concentration by supplier/channel.

## What not to do

- Publish products with an unknown supplier or unknown fulfilment path.
- Treat Klarna retail results as supplier inventory.
- Claim a margin before shipping and other direct costs are known.
- Buy large inventory before demand is proven.
- Publish hundreds of thin AI-generated pages as the primary growth strategy.
- Claim products were physically tested when they were not.
- Depend on one supplier, retailer or affiliate network.
- Fabricate tracking links, stock status, delivery times, reviews or discounts.
